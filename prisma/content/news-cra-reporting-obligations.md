The first major operational obligations of the **EU Cyber Resilience Act (CRA)** apply from **September 11, 2026**. From this date, manufacturers must report **actively exploited vulnerabilities** and **severe incidents** affecting the security of their products with digital elements.

For the industrial automation sector, the change is significant. Controllers, HMIs, industrial switches and gateways, remote I/O, drives with network interfaces and engineering software are generally products with digital elements within the regulation's scope when they are made available on the EU market.

## What the regulation requires now

Under Article 14 of Regulation (EU) 2024/2847, manufacturers must notify both events through a single reporting platform, with notifications going to the CSIRT designated as coordinator in the relevant member state and to ENISA, the EU cybersecurity agency.

| Step | Actively exploited vulnerability | Severe incident |
|---|---|---|
| Early warning | Within 24 hours of becoming aware | Within 24 hours of becoming aware |
| Notification | Within 72 hours, with available details and mitigations | Within 72 hours, with initial assessment |
| Final report | Within 14 days after a corrective or mitigating measure is available | Within one month after the incident notification |

Manufacturers must also inform affected users about the vulnerability or incident and, where necessary, about mitigation and corrective measures they can deploy.

Importantly, the reporting obligations apply to products already on the market — not only to products placed on the market after the CRA's main obligations take effect.

## Key dates

- **December 10, 2024:** The CRA entered into force.
- **June 11, 2026:** Provisions on conformity assessment bodies began to apply.
- **September 11, 2026:** Vulnerability and incident reporting obligations apply.
- **December 11, 2027:** The remaining obligations apply, including the essential cybersecurity requirements, conformity assessment and CE marking for cybersecurity.

## What this means for automation manufacturers

The reporting deadlines are short by industrial standards. Many OT vendors already run product security incident response teams (PSIRTs) and publish advisories, but a 24-hour early warning requires that detection, triage, legal review and submission work reliably — including on weekends.

Practical steps:

1. **Establish or review your PSIRT process** so that potential exploitation reports reach a decision-maker quickly.
2. **Define clear criteria** for "actively exploited" and "severe incident", documented and agreed with legal and product teams.
3. **Prepare submission templates and roles** for the 24-hour, 72-hour and final reports.
4. **Maintain software bills of materials (SBOMs)** so you can quickly determine which products contain an affected component.
5. **Align with existing frameworks.** A secure development lifecycle according to IEC 62443-4-1 already covers much of the vulnerability handling process the CRA expects.

## What it means for asset owners

Plant operators are not directly subject to manufacturer reporting duties, but they should expect more timely and structured security notifications from suppliers. It is a good moment to check that the right people in your organization are subscribed to vendor advisories and that you can map advisories to your installed base.

## Penalties

Non-compliance with the essential requirements and the manufacturer obligations, including reporting, can lead to administrative fines of up to €15 million or 2.5% of total worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher.

=== takeaways
EU Cyber Resilience Act reporting obligations for manufacturers apply from September 11, 2026.
Manufacturers must give an early warning within 24 hours of becoming aware of an actively exploited vulnerability or severe incident, followed by a notification within 72 hours.
Reports go through a single reporting platform to the coordinating national CSIRT and to ENISA.
The reporting obligations also apply to products with digital elements that were placed on the EU market before December 2027.
The CRA's remaining obligations, including essential cybersecurity requirements and CE marking, apply from December 11, 2027.

=== faqs
Q: When do Cyber Resilience Act reporting obligations start?
A: The CRA's vulnerability and incident reporting obligations for manufacturers apply from September 11, 2026.

Q: Do CRA reporting obligations apply to PLCs and industrial equipment?
A: Industrial products with digital elements — such as PLCs, HMIs, network devices and engineering software — that are made available on the EU market are generally within the CRA's scope, so their manufacturers are subject to the reporting obligations. Products covered by certain sector-specific EU rules may be excluded.

Q: What is the deadline for reporting an actively exploited vulnerability under the CRA?
A: An early warning must be submitted within 24 hours of the manufacturer becoming aware, a vulnerability notification within 72 hours, and a final report no later than 14 days after a corrective or mitigating measure is available.
