The global installed base of industrial robots in factories has crossed **5 million operational units** for the first time, according to the **World Robotics 2026** statistics released by the **International Federation of Robotics (IFR)** in September 2026 and expanded with new global **robot density** figures on **November 12, 2026**.

Factories worldwide installed **more than 600,000 new industrial robots in 2025** — an 11% jump over the prior year — lifting total operational stock by 9% to a record 5 million units, more than double the installed base seven years earlier. Looking ahead, the IFR forecasts annual installations to rise another **9% to 655,000 units in 2026** and reach **806,000 units by 2029**.

## Regional market shifts: US overtakes Japan for second place

The 2025 installation figures reveal diverging regional momentum across Asia, the Americas and Europe:

| Market | 2025 Installations | YoY Change | Global Rank (Installations) |
|---|---|---|---|
| China | 354,000 | +20% | 1st (59% global share) |
| United States | ~38,500 | +12% | 2nd (overtook Japan) |
| Japan | 36,219 | -19% | 3rd |
| South Korea | 30,000 | -1% | 4th |
| Germany | <25,000 | -8% | 5th (41% of EU total) |
| India | ~10,500 | +15% | 6th (27% CAGR 2020–2025) |

**China** widened its lead as the world's largest industrial robotics market, installing **354,000 units** in 2025 — accounting for **59% of all global deployments** and beating its previous annual record by nearly 60,000 robots. Domestic Chinese robot manufacturers again outsold foreign rivals in their home market, delivering 195,000 units (+15%) for a **55% domestic market share** (compared with 57% in 2024).

In the Americas, the **United States** grew 12% to nearly **38,500 installations**, overtaking **Japan** (36,219 units, down 19%) to become the world's second-largest annual market. **India** climbed to sixth place globally with roughly 10,500 installations (+15%), extending a five-year compound annual growth rate of 27%. Meanwhile, **Brazil** surged 38% to nearly 4,300 units as automotive robot deployments rose 212%, driven largely by investments from Chinese automakers.

**Europe** experienced a softer investment cycle: **Germany** remained Europe's dominant market with 41% of EU installations, though annual volume dipped 8% to fewer than 25,000 units as traditional automotive spending cooled.

## November 2026 robot density data: South Korea tops 1,066

In its follow-up density release on **November 12, 2026**, the IFR reported that the **global average manufacturing robot density** reached a record **152 industrial robots per 10,000 employees**.

Five of the world's ten most automated manufacturing economies are EU member states: **Germany, Sweden, Denmark, Slovenia and the Netherlands**. China's rapid industrial build-out pushed its density up **147% between 2020 and 2025** to 193 units per 10,000 employees, recalculated using China's latest National Economic Census workforce figures.

## What the numbers mean for plant engineers and integrators

Beyond headline volumes, the IFR highlights three structural forces shaping automation engineering through 2029:

1. **Reshoring and labor scarcity:** Manufacturing relocation into higher-wage regions and chronic shortages of skilled welders, machine tenders and palletizing staff are sustaining robot budgets even where macroeconomic growth is uneven.
2. **Lower integration barriers:** Improved machine vision, AI-assisted path planning and simplified programming interfaces are moving robots out of fixed high-volume automotive lines into high-mix metalworking, electronics, food and logistics cells.
3. **Lifecycle and safety compliance demands:** With 5 million robots already operating in factories — and the [EU Machinery Regulation (EU) 2023/1230 taking effect on January 20, 2027](/news/eu-machinery-regulation-2023-1230-january-2027) — plant teams face growing demand for standardized [PLC-to-robot cell integration](/blog/robot-cell-integration-plc-safety-fieldbus-guide), cyber-resilient safety controls and reliable spare-parts lifecycle planning.
