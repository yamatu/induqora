The **EU Machinery Regulation (EU) 2023/1230** applies from **January 20, 2027**, replacing the Machinery Directive 2006/42/EC that has governed machine safety in Europe since 2009. Machines placed on the EU market from that date must comply with the new regulation.

Because it is a **regulation** rather than a directive, it applies directly in all member states without national transposition — removing differences in how countries implemented the old directive.

## The main changes

### Safety requirements that address cybersecurity

The regulation's essential health and safety requirements now explicitly cover **protection against corruption**:

- Connecting another device to the machine, including remotely, must not lead to a hazardous situation.
- Hardware and software components critical to safety must be protected against accidental or intentional corruption.
- Machines must collect evidence of legitimate or illegitimate interventions in safety-relevant software and its configuration.
- Safety-related control systems must be designed so that malicious attempts by third parties cannot lead to hazardous situations.

For automation engineers, this is the most consequential change: network access, remote maintenance, and the integrity of safety PLC programs and parameters are now part of the safety case. Standards such as IEC 62443 are expected to play a growing role alongside traditional functional safety standards like ISO 13849 and IEC 62061.

### Self-evolving behavior and AI

Safety components and machinery with embedded systems that have **fully or partially self-evolving behavior using machine learning** to ensure safety functions are listed among the high-risk categories that require third-party conformity assessment by a notified body.

### Digital instructions

Instructions for use may be supplied in **digital format**. However, if the customer requests it at the time of purchase, the manufacturer must provide a paper version free of charge. The EU declaration of conformity may also be provided digitally.

### Substantial modification

A person who **substantially modifies** a machine — in a way not foreseen by the original manufacturer that affects safety by creating a new hazard or increasing an existing risk — is treated as a manufacturer and takes on the corresponding obligations. This is particularly relevant for retrofit projects and integrators upgrading control systems.

### Updated list of high-risk machinery

The list of high-risk machinery categories has been revised. For a subset of categories, manufacturers can no longer self-certify and must involve a notified body, regardless of whether harmonized standards are applied.

## What machine builders should do now

1. **Review your product portfolio** against the new high-risk categories.
2. **Update risk assessments** to include cybersecurity threats that could affect safety functions, particularly remote access and network interfaces.
3. **Protect safety-related software and parameters** with access control, integrity checks and change logging.
4. **Update documentation workflows** to support digital instructions and declarations of conformity.
5. **Track harmonized standards** being developed or revised for the new regulation, since they provide the presumption of conformity.
6. **Coordinate with the Cyber Resilience Act.** Many connected machines will also fall under the CRA, whose main obligations apply from December 2027.

=== faqs
Q: When does the EU Machinery Regulation 2023/1230 apply?
A: The Machinery Regulation applies from January 20, 2027. From that date it replaces the Machinery Directive 2006/42/EC.

Q: Does the Machinery Regulation include cybersecurity requirements?
A: Yes. Its essential health and safety requirements include protection against corruption, requiring that safety-related hardware, software and data are protected against accidental or intentional corruption and that malicious third-party attempts cannot lead to hazardous situations.

Q: Can machine instructions be provided only in digital form?
A: Instructions may be provided digitally, but the manufacturer must supply a paper version free of charge if the customer requests it at the time of purchase.
